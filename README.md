# The Impact of Technology on Sleep Among Different Age Groups

## Project Overview

This project investigates the relationship between **technology use and sleep quality** among people of different age groups.

The study focuses on electronic devices in the bedroom, activities before bedtime, sleep duration, sleep quality, sleep disturbances, and people's perceptions of how technology affects their sleep.

The project was conducted as part of an English-language academic assignment and included:

* an online questionnaire;
* 165 participants;
* comparison of different age groups;
* analysis of questionnaire responses;
* background research based on previous studies;
* presentation slides summarizing the research and findings.

---

## Aim of the Study

The main aim of this study was to examine the **impact of technology on sleep quality** and to determine whether this impact differs between age groups.

---

## Research Questions

1. **How does technology affect sleep? Does it affect it at all?**
2. **Does the impact of technology on sleep differ between age groups?**

---

## Hypothesis

The hypothesis was that technology use before sleep would be associated with negative effects on sleep quality and duration.

It was also expected that the relationship would differ between age groups and that participants with greater technology use before sleep would be more likely to experience sleep disturbances.

---

## Background

The study was based on previous research examining the relationship between technology use and sleep.

### National Sleep Foundation – Sleep in America Poll

The study *The Sleep and Technology Use of Americans: Findings from the National Sleep Foundation's 2011 Sleep in America Poll* reported that approximately nine out of ten Americans used some type of technological device during the hour before going to bed.

Younger participants were more likely to use cell phones before sleep than older participants. Interactive devices, including computers, laptops, cell phones, and video game consoles, were associated with greater reports of difficulty falling asleep and unrefreshing sleep.

### Bedtime Mobile Phone Use and Sleep in Adults

The study *Bedtime mobile phone use and sleep in adults* examined mobile phone use around bedtime.

Its findings suggested that activities such as sending messages and making phone calls after lights out were associated with longer sleep latency, lower sleep efficiency, more sleep disturbances, and greater daytime dysfunction. Some of these relationships also varied by age.

### Technology Use and Sleep Quality in Adults

The 2022 study *The impact of bedtime technology use on sleep quality and excessive daytime sleepiness in adults* examined more than 10,000 adults in Saudi Arabia.

The study reported an association between regular use of many electronic devices at bedtime and poorer self-reported sleep quality.

### Technology Use in Adolescents

The study *Technology Use and Sleep Quality in Preadolescence and Adolescence* examined 850 participants aged 11–16.

The researchers reported that evening and nighttime electronic media use was associated with later bedtimes, shorter total sleep time, and poorer sleep quality.

These studies provided the background for the questionnaire used in this project.

---

## Methodology

### Research Design

A quantitative questionnaire-based approach was used.

Data were collected through an **anonymous online questionnaire** over a period of one week.

The questionnaire included closed-ended and open-ended questions covering:

* demographic information;
* bedtime;
* average sleep duration;
* activities before sleep;
* number of electronic devices in the bedroom;
* perceived effect of electronic devices on sleep quality;
* sleep disorders;
* dreams and morning habits;
* daytime well-being;
* perceived impact of technology on sleep;
* willingness to change sleep routines.

### Participants

A total of **165 participants** completed the questionnaire.

Participants were divided into age groups to examine whether perceptions and sleep-related responses differed between age groups.

---

## Questionnaire

The questionnaire was created using **Google Forms**.

It consisted of seven sections, including demographic questions, sleep-related questions, questions about technology and sleep, and follow-up open-ended questions.

### Main Questionnaire Questions

**Demographic information**

* What is your gender?
* What is your age group?
* What do you do?
* If you work, what is your occupation?

**Sleep and technology**

* What time do you usually go to sleep?
* How many hours do you sleep on average?
* What do you usually do before going to sleep?
* How many electronic devices do you have in your bedroom?
* Do electronic devices affect your sleep quality?
* If yes, please explain your answer.

**Sleep disorders**

* Do you have sleep disorders?
* If yes, please explain your answer.

**Dreams and morning habits**

* How often do you dream?
* What are your dreams usually like?
* What do you usually do when you wake up in the morning?
* How do you get up in the morning?
* Do you often wake up feeling well-rested?
* What is your usual condition during the day?

**Perceived impact of technology**

* How much impact do you think technology has on sleep?

This question used a scale from **1 to 5**, where 1 represented a very small impact and 5 represented a very large impact.

* Would you like to change your sleep routine?

---

## Data Analysis

The questionnaire responses were analyzed using **Excel**.

The analysis included:

* counting responses;
* calculating percentages;
* comparing responses between age groups;
* examining general patterns between technology use and sleep-related responses.

The analysis was descriptive and focused on identifying patterns within the collected sample.

Because the data were self-reported, the results represent participants' reported experiences and perceptions.

---

## Results

The initial hypothesis expected greater technology use before sleep to be associated with poorer sleep quality and shorter sleep duration.

However, the questionnaire results did **not clearly support this hypothesis**.

Approximately **80% of respondents reported that they do not experience problems with sleeping**.

Participants generally reported getting enough sleep, with many reporting **7 or more hours of sleep per day**.

The results also did not show a clear difference in reported sleep disturbances between the analysed age groups.

Overall, participants who reported greater technology use did not show a clearly higher likelihood of sleep disturbances based on the questionnaire responses.

---

## Differences Between Age Groups

Although clear differences in reported sleep disturbances were not observed between age groups, differences were found in participants' **perceptions of technology's influence on sleep**.

Younger participants generally reported a stronger perceived influence of technology on sleep, with responses around **3–5 on the five-point scale**, where 5 represented the strongest perceived effect.

Approximately **70% of younger participants** also expressed a desire to change their sleep routines.

Older participants showed different perceptions of technology's influence on sleep and different levels of willingness to change their sleep habits.

These results suggest that people's **perception of technology's impact** does not necessarily correspond to their **reported sleep problems**.

---

## Interpretation of the Results

The findings should be interpreted with caution because the study used:

* a sample of 165 participants;
* an online questionnaire;
* self-reported information;
* descriptive analysis rather than an experimental design.

Therefore, the results cannot establish that technology directly causes poor sleep.

The study describes patterns and perceptions within the collected sample rather than demonstrating a causal relationship.

## Tips for Improving Bedtime Habits

Several practical recommendations were considered for people who want to reduce technology use before sleep.

### 1. Keep electronics away from the bed

If possible, keep electronic devices outside the bedroom or away from the sleeping area.

### 2. Avoid technology before bedtime

Try to avoid electronic devices for approximately one hour before going to sleep.

### 3. Silence notifications

Using Airplane Mode, Do Not Disturb, or silent mode can reduce interruptions during the night.

### 4. Choose a non-electronic activity

Reading a physical book or magazine can be an alternative to using a phone or tablet before bed.

### 5. Avoid checking devices during the night

If you wake up, try to avoid immediately checking your phone or other electronic devices.

### 6. Make gradual changes

Changing bedtime habits gradually and setting small goals can make the process easier to maintain.
